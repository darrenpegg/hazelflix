Installing it

HazelFlix comes as a folder, from its releases. It's one JavaScript file run by Node.js, with nothing to install from npm. You'll need:

  • Node.js 22.5 or later,
  • ffmpeg and ffprobe (on a Mac, brew install ffmpeg),
  • curl and sqlite3, which come with a Mac,
  • optionally, an ffmpeg that can draw subtitles, for subtitles in the picture on TVs that don't show them (on a Mac, brew install ffmpeg-full: it's kept beside ffmpeg, and HazelFlix finds it).

Then, in the folder:

./start.sh --library ~/Movies

and open http://127.0.0.1:8095 on the same computer.

Reaching it from your phone and TVs

Started as above, only the computer it's on can reach it. To use it from your phone and send films to your TVs, start it listening on your network:

./start.sh --web 0.0.0.0:8095 --library ~/Movies

Then, from your phone, open http://this-computer.local:8095 (the computer's name, then .local), or scan the code on HazelFlix on your phone, and put it on your home screen.

OptionWhat it's for
--web HOST:PORTwhere it listens. 127.0.0.1:8095 (the default) is this computer only; 0.0.0.0:8095 is your whole network, which your phone and TVs need
--library FOLDERa folder of films, series or music, as many as you like (you can add more in the page, and make them into libraries)
--db PATHits database, with the rest of its data beside it
--quietless in the log

In Docker

The folder has a Dockerfile; its README.md says how to build and run it. TVs find HazelFlix by multicast, so on Linux it needs --network host.

Your data, and updating

Your data (the database, settings, keys, photos, posters and converted copies) is kept outside the folder: on a Mac in ~/Library/Application Support/HazelFlix, on Linux in ~/.local/share/hazelflix, or wherever HAZELFLIX_DATA says. So to update, replace the folder with the new one and start it again. Pages left open on your phone, laptop or a Ready to play screen load the new version by themselves (once nothing on them is playing).

Back up your data folder before updating: a new version may change the database as it starts, and there's no going back.

The first time

The first profile, Owner, is made for you, and with one profile and no code HazelFlix goes straight in. Rename it on Your profile (the circle at the top right). Only the owner changes Settings. More on profiles.

The home page has a film at the top, then Continue watching, what's been added to each library, TV shows, the top rated, a row for each genre, and one of your collections (its name opens it; All collections lists them all). The film at the top and the collection change every ten minutes.

Your first library

Open Libraries (from Your profile, or Settings), and under A new library:

  1. give it a name ("Films"),
  2. choose what it holds (Films and TV reads each file's name to tell; Films, TV, Music and Photos say so),
  3. give its first folder, such as /Volumes/media/Videos. A share on your network has to be mounted in Finder first; it's then under /Volumes.

Make it, and HazelFlix scans it straight away. Each video found is looked at (its length, its picture, its sound) and then looked up. More in Libraries.

The engines: finding what your films are

HazelFlix reads a file's name for its title and year (Die Hard (1988) 1080p.mkv is Die Hard, 1988), then asks the engines what it is: its poster, plot, people and ratings. In Settings, Engines:

  • TMDB gives posters, backdrops, people's photos, collections and TV seasons. It needs a key: Get a key takes you to where it's free, then paste it in.
  • OMDb adds ratings (IMDb's, Rotten Tomatoes', Metacritic's). It needs a key too.
  • IMDb is IMDb's own datasets, downloaded and kept here (Fetch now; a few hundred MB). No key, and nothing asked over the network once it's here, but no pictures.

Their order is the order they're asked in. A library can also be looked up in IMDb's datasets alone, or not at all: see indexing.

What no engine finds is under Unmatched (Settings), to be found by hand.

Converting

Browsers play only some files as they are (MP4 with H.264, mostly). The rest play live, made playable as they play, which takes the computer's effort and can't skip ahead as freely. Converting (Settings) makes a browser-ready copy of each in the background, one or two at a time, and a converted file plays like any other. You can pause it, and a library can be left out of it. More in Playing.
