Here, or on another screen

Press Play (or Resume) on a film or episode. When another screen is ready, you're asked Play where?: this browser, or a screen by name.

A screen is:

  • a browser set to Play (Settings, Browsers), showing Ready to play. Tap it once (browsers need that before they'll play sound), and it waits for a film.
  • a DLNA TV or player ticked under Players on the network in Settings.

Play where? lists only the screens that can play it: a TV whose profile doesn't see the film isn't offered (see TVs). With none left, it plays where you are. On a series, an episode's picture plays it too. A series' episodes show as a list or a grid: List and Grid beside the season tabs, remembered in this browser for every series.

The remote

Remote, at the top: the screens as tabs, what each is playing, and its controls: pause, skip, stop, the sound, and a bar to seek with. It opens on the screen that's playing, unless you choose another.

Converting

A browser plays some files as they are. The rest play live, made playable as they go, marked live on their poster. Converting (Settings) makes a copy each browser plays, in the background, so live is only until it's done. Press Convert on a film's file to move it to the front.

Each browser and player can be given a format (Settings, Browsers and Players): what it's sent, made as it plays and kept if that's ticked. On Default it's sent the file if it plays it, else the best copy there is, else a stream.

Subtitles and sound

A film's subtitles are those beside its file and named as it is, with a language after if you like (Film.srt, Film.en.srt; any name at all when the film has a folder of its own), and those HazelFlix fetches from OpenSubtitles. To fetch them, open Settings, Subtitles:

  1. Make a free key: sign in at OpenSubtitles, open Get a key (API consumers), add one, and paste its key in.
  2. Optionally, add your OpenSubtitles username and password. Without them the key allows a few downloads a day; with them, your account's allowance.
  3. List the languages you want, in order, by OpenSubtitles' codes: en, fr, pt-br.

A film's subtitles are fetched when you open its page or play it, and an episode's (and the next one's) when it plays, so they're usually there by the time you press Play. Once fetched they're kept, and never fetched again.

The first language's subtitles show as a film starts, unless you untick that; the player's subtitles button chooses another or turns them off. Their font, size, colour, background, corners (square, or one box with rounded corners) and position (title safe, a tenth of the picture above its bottom edge as TV's are, at first; higher; or the bottom edge) are set there too, and the preview shows them as you choose. A phone playing full screen, and a TV, show subtitles their own way. A TV is offered the film's subtitles (your first language's) and turns them on and off from its own menu (Samsung's say they take them; others vary, and an Xbox may show none). For those, Your profile can have them drawn into the picture.

A film's sound track is the language in Settings, Audio, or the one chosen on its Edit page.
