HazelFlix

Your films, series, music and photos, on every screen in the house.

DownloadView on GitHub

Made to be run from your phone

HazelFlix isn't a media server you browse on the TV with its remote: your phone is the remote. Find a film on your phone, press Play, choose a screen (the TV in the living room, the projector, a laptop's browser) and it plays there, while your phone shows what's playing, with pause, seek and the sound.

Your films live in one place, your phone chooses, and any screen plays. Each screen only plays what its profile allows, so the kids' TV only ever plays the kids' films, whoever sends them.

What it does

Your library

Point it at your folders of films and series. It finds them, with their posters, cast, ratings and seasons, and shows them the way a streaming service would.

Every screen

Play in any browser, or send to the TVs and games consoles on your network (DLNA). Whatever a screen can't play is converted for it as it plays.

Music

Your music library too, by album and artist. Sent to a TV, each song gets a card on the screen: its cover, its title, the artist and the album.

Photos as a screen saver

Upload photos from your phone, iPhone ones included. Every idle screen shows them, moving gently, with where and when each one was taken.

A remote in your pocket

Open it on your phone to choose what plays on which screen, pause it, seek and turn it up, from the sofa.

Yours alone

It runs on your own machine: no account, no cloud, no subscription. Your library and your photos never leave the house.

Yak shaving

HazelFlix is really a test of a compiler suite.

It's written in lsp, a Scheme-like language whose programs compile to native code, JavaScript and C#. The release is what lsp made of it for Node.js: one JavaScript file. It's a real program because a real program finds what a test suite doesn't, so expect it to be useful, but not a product.
