Privacy

Last changed 18 November 2026.

HazelFlix runs on your own computer, and we never see any of it. There's no account, no cloud service, no tracking and no analytics. Nothing about you, your library or how you use it is ever sent to us. We don't have a server for it to go to.

What stays on your machines

Everything HazelFlix keeps stays on the computer you run it on: your films, series, music and photos, what it finds out about them, your profiles and their codes, what each person has watched, your playlists, your settings and your keys. Your phone, your TVs and your browsers talk to that computer over your own network, and to nothing else of ours.

What it asks others, and when

To find out what your films are, HazelFlix can ask a few outside services. Each library chooses, in its settings: found by the network engines (a new library's choice, though they send nothing until you've given them a key), found in the local IMDb, or named from their files (which asks no one at all).

The network engines: TMDB and OMDb

With a library's engines set to the network, HazelFlix sends the title and year it reads from a file's name (Die Hard (1988)), never the file, its path or anything else about you, and gets back what that film or series is: its poster, plot, people, ratings and seasons. To keep things up to date it later asks by the film's own id, and for the people, collections and seasons that came with it.

  • TMDB (The Movie Database) answers with the details and the pictures. HazelFlix downloads the pictures itself and keeps them, so your browser never fetches them from TMDB.
  • OMDb adds ratings, asked by the film's IMDb id (or, if you've put it first in the engines' order, by the title and year, as TMDB is).

Both need a key, which is yours: you get it from them and put it in HazelFlix's Settings. As with any website your computer talks to, they see your computer's internet address. Their own privacy policies say what they do with requests.

IMDb's datasets, kept on your computer

The local IMDb is IMDb's public datasets, downloaded once to your computer (a few hundred MB). Films are then looked up there, on your machine, and nothing about them is sent anywhere. HazelFlix fetches the datasets again every 7 days to keep them current (a setting), or when you press Fetch now. Each fetch is a download of the whole set, the same for everyone, which says nothing about what's in your library.

The rest, and when

  • Wikidata and Wikipedia: the short biography and photo of the people in your films, a few at a time in the background, or when a person's page is first opened. Asked by the person's IMDb id, with HazelFlix named as asking.
  • MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive: an album's cover, when it has none of its own. Asked by the artist's and album's names, from the songs' tags.
  • GeoNames: a list of the world's towns, downloaded once, so a photo's place is found on your computer. Its location is never sent anywhere.
  • YouTube: only if you press Trailer. That opens the trailer on YouTube, in your browser, as any link would.

A library named from its files asks none of these about its films: its titles come from the file names, its posters from a frame of each film.

Your photos

Photos you upload from your phone go to your HazelFlix, and nowhere else. They're made ready (resized, and turned from HEIC where needed) on your computer.

This website

hazelflix.com is a handful of plain pages: no cookies, no scripts, no analytics, nothing to sign up for. It's hosted by GitHub Pages, and the download comes from GitHub, so GitHub sees those visits as it sees any. GitHub's privacy statement says what it keeps.
